The opportunity

MAN's entry into the AI age begins on the customer desk.

MAN owns no ships or aircraft — it sells coordination, visibility and responsiveness, backed by a 15-year reputation for exactly that. An AI agent layer on the customer desk answers shipment questions in seconds, alerts clients before they ask, and routes the hard cases to the right person — so that reputation scales without scaling headcount. Freight forwarding nets 3–4 paise per rupee invoiced, so every rupee of value created here carries the weight of 25–30 rupees of freight revenue.

Why it pays
Pillar 01 · Step into the AI age first POSITION

First mover takes the whole prize.

No mid-size Indian forwarder has put AI on the customer desk yet. Whoever moves first owns the story, the RFP wins and the pricing power.

AI-in-logistics ~31.7% / yr
Pillar 02 · Retention is the return RETURNS

Keeping a client beats winning one.

An always-on, 24/7 desk removes the service slips that quietly lose long-tenure accounts as volume grows. Protected revenue is the highest-leverage rupee on the P&L.

5–7× cheaper than acquisition
Pillar 03 · Stickiness by design MOAT

Tell clients first, and they stay.

Proactive alerts embed MAN into the client's daily workflow — and every interaction becomes data that makes the service sharper. Embedded experience is hard to leave.

WISMO volume down 50–70%

The bottom line

Every lever above is hard cost, freed cash or protected revenue — value that lands on the bottom line like profit. That's why a crore of it rivals tens of crores of new freight: at a 3–4% net margin, normal sales keep only 3–4 paise per rupee. And the money is just the floor — first-mover position, the returned time and the compounding data advantage all sit on top, uncounted here.
